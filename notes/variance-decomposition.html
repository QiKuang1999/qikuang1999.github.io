Method Note 01 · Multilevel models

Country, school, or classroom? Variance decomposition of teacher outcomes

TALIS 2024 · multilevel models · intraclass correlation · BRR weights

When teacher outcomes — job satisfaction, self-efficacy, working conditions — differ, a natural policy question is where the differences live. Are teachers in some countries systematically better off than in others? Do outcomes vary mostly between schools within a country, so that which school you work in matters most? Or is most of the variation between teachers inside the same school, where system-level levers have little direct grip?

A multilevel variance decomposition answers this by splitting total variance into components at each level. For the TALIS Principals Report, I ran these decompositions for teacher outcomes across country, school, and within-school (teacher) levels, and translated the shares into policy analysis and ISCED-level content.

The setup

The workhorse is an "empty" (intercept-only) three-level model: teachers nested in schools nested in countries, with a random intercept at each level. The estimated variance components give the share of variation attributable to each level, and the intraclass correlation (ICC) tells you how similar two teachers from the same school (or country) are relative to two teachers drawn at random. A large school-level share points to sorting and school-context effects; a dominant within-school share says most of the story plays out among colleagues in the same building.

What makes it tricky in practice

Weights at every level. TALIS samples schools first and teachers within them, with unequal probabilities. Multilevel models need weights applied — and appropriately scaled — at each level; getting the scaling wrong biases the variance components, especially the school-level one. Uncertainty then has to respect the design, which is where the BRR replicate weights come in.

Unbalanced samples. The number of sampled teachers per school is small and uneven (often around twenty), so school-level variance is estimated from noisy school means. Part of what looks like "school variance" can be sampling noise, and the decomposition needs to be read with that in mind.

Few countries, many teachers. The country level has far fewer units than the other levels, which makes the country-level variance component the least precisely estimated. Treating countries as fixed rather than random, or presenting decompositions within countries, is sometimes the more defensible choice depending on the question.

Shares are descriptive, not causal. A small between-school share does not mean schools don't matter; it means current differences between schools explain little of the outcome's variation. Composition, measurement error in self-reported scales, and cross-country differences in how constructs are understood all flow into the shares.

The decompositions fed directly into the TALIS 2024 Principals Report, alongside ISCED-level breakdowns, to help readers see at which level of the system differences in teacher outcomes actually arise.

Implementation

Estimation runs in R inside the standardised TALIS workflow, with results tables and StatLinks produced by the same code that builds the rest of the report — so every number in the text can be traced back to a reproducible script.
