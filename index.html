Profile

Economist by training, data engineer by practice.

My research interests centre on inequality, social mobility, and resilience to climate and institutional shocks. As an applied economist, I combine administrative data, household surveys, and satellite and geospatial information to study how public policies and external shocks shape individual and institutional outcomes — and I build the data infrastructure that makes those analyses reproducible.

At the OECD I currently work on TALIS (Teaching and Learning International Survey), using R, Python, and other software for data analysis and producing the statistical tables and charts behind the TALIS 2024 reports, while developing new analytical methods — including multilevel variance decomposition and weighted Lorenz curves. Before that I worked on Education at a Glance and the UOE data collection, managing questionnaires, building R and Stata processing tools, and leading a school-level data project with geospatial analysis — experience that made international education statistics one of my strongest domains.

I hold a master's in economics (Analysis and Policy in Economics) from the Paris School of Economics, where I graduated with honours as a French government scholarship holder, and a bachelor's in economics from Zhejiang University.
